Skater radar

Every component below is compared against a pooled set of games (all regular-season games, or all playoff games, of whatever season is selected) rather than just the games in view — this keeps the comparison stable instead of being skewed by a single freak game early in a small sample.

⚔ Offence
Goals 48% Assists 32% Shots 20% + Penalties Drawn bonus (capped)

Goals, assists and shots on goal are each normalized 0–10 on their own, then blended by the weights above. Before normalizing, every game's raw numbers get two multipliers applied:

  • Position multiplier — Goals/Assists: Forwards ×0.784, Defense ×1.387 (derived from real top-scorer output by position). Shots: Forwards ×0.86, Defense ×1.34 (derived from this roster's own shot-volume rates). Position is read from what a player actually lined up as that specific game (the line-slot letters), not their usual roster position — so a defenseman filling in at forward for a night gets the forward multiplier that game.
  • Line multiplier — Line 1 and 2 both ×1.0 (roughly equal ice time in practice, so no adjustment between them); Line 3 ×2.1, since 3rd-liners produce a lot less, grounded in this team's own scoring gap (dampened from the raw 2.92x gap observed).

Assists are combined into one weighted value before normalizing — not kept as separate primary/secondary components — so a player heavy on secondary assists isn't unfairly penalized: primary assist = 1.0, secondary = 0.538 (same ratio, just anchored differently).

Offence = 0.48 × Goals(0-10) + 0.32 × Assists(0-10) + 0.20 × Shots(0-10) + Penalties-Drawn bonus (0 to +1, capped so total never exceeds 10)
🎯 Shooting
Forwards: Shot% 70% / SOG% 30% Defense: Shot% 40% / SOG% 60%

Finishing (Shot%) matters more for forwards; getting the puck through traffic (SOG%) matters more for defensemen, hence the different split by position.

  • Shot% uses a fixed real-world range — 2% (bad) to 18% (elite) — not roster comparison, based on real NHL shooting benchmarks (league average sits near the midpoint, ~9.5%).
  • SOG% (shots on goal ÷ shot attempts) is compared against the pool, since there's no equivalent external benchmark for it.
🛡 Defence
GA-against 40% Blocks 40% Team Shot Diff 20%

All three use fixed ranges, not roster comparison — each grounded in either real data or this team's own observed spread:

  • GA-against (inverted — lower is better): goals allowed while on ice. Penalty-kill and empty-net goals against are excluded entirely where we have confirmed which specific goals those were (from real game-by-game tagging); a shorthanded goal scored against the team counts more (1.25x), since that's a bigger individual lapse. A line multiplier also applies (Line 3 allows more, reflecting more ice time and tougher matchups for the top lines) — regular season: 0.88 / 0.98 / 1.19; playoffs: 0.78 / 1.00 / 1.50 (benches shorten in playoff hockey). Fixed range: 0.55 (good) to 1.2 (bad), cross-checked against real NHL GA/60 benchmarks scaled down to an amateur ~15-minute game.
  • Blocks get a position multiplier (Forwards ×1.44, Defense ×0.68, since blocks are rarer for forwards) and a fixed range of 0 to 1.5 blocks/game.
  • Team Shot Differential — the team's shots-for minus shots-against in games this specific player appeared in, averaged per game. This is a coarser "team performance when this player plays" signal, not true individual on-ice tracking (the sheet doesn't record that), which is why it carries a smaller 20% share. Fixed range: -8 to +12.
✋ Discipline

PIM per game, inverted (fewer penalties = better), compared only against same-position peers — forwards vs. forwards, defensemen vs. defensemen — since the two take penalties at different rates for different reasons.

⭕ Faceoffs

Only shown for players who've actually taken draws. Faceoff win % on a fixed range: 40% (bad) to 60% (elite).

Goalie radar

All three goalie benchmarks below come from the real OlyBet EHL E5 league leaderboard (across the 2024-25 and 2025-26 seasons, both regular season and playoffs) — not this roster's own goalies, and not an NHL comparison.

🥅 Save %

Fixed range, split by season type since real league data showed playoff SV% runs meaningfully higher (tighter, more defensive playoff hockey):

  • Regular season: 82% (bad) to 91% (elite)
  • Playoffs: 86% (bad) to 93% (elite)

Both derived from the real league's 10th/90th percentile split, filtered to goalies with a meaningful sample (5+ GP for season entries, 3+ GP for playoffs).

✅ Quality Start %

The share of games where a goalie's own SV% that specific game met or beat the real league median for that season type — 87.8% in the regular season, 89.7% in playoffs — the same methodology Rob Vollman's original NHL "Quality Start" stat uses, just applied with this league's real threshold instead of the NHL's. Scored on a fixed range of 30% to 80%.

📊 Consistency

The downside deviation of a goalie's per-game SV% — not full standard deviation. Only games below their own baseline count toward the penalty; a shutout or any great game above it contributes zero, since "inconsistent" should mean having bad nights, not good ones.

The baseline itself excludes shutout games when computing the average — otherwise a single shutout would pull the average up and make ordinary good games look "below average" when they aren't really bad nights at all.

baseline = average of all non-shutout games (or all games, if every game was a shutout) downside deviation = √( average of min(0, game SV% - baseline)² across all games )

Scored on a fixed range of 0 (perfectly steady) to 5.0 percentage points (erratic) — grounded in this team's own observed spread across its goalies, not the league file, since the league data only has season-level totals for other teams, not per-game detail to compute a real spread from. Scored on the goalie's own numbers only, not compared against other goalies — with only a handful of goalies on one roster, that comparison was too noisy to be meaningful.
